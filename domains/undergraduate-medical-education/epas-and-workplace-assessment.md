# UME Domain: EPAs & Workplace-Based Assessment

How **Entrustable Professional Activities (EPAs)** and **workplace-based assessment (WBA)** map onto the
Learning Signal Check chain. This file owns the EPA/WBA detail; the
[`domain-pack.md`](domain-pack.md) gives the UME overview.

**This file does not re-author the diagnostic.** It applies the canonical five-link chain from
[`../../core/diagnostic-procedure.md`](../../core/diagnostic-procedure.md) to one specific family of UME
assessments. The links, their order, the halting rule, severity, and readiness all live in the keystone
and are used here unchanged. The repairs named below are pointers into
[`../../core/repair-taxonomy.md`](../../core/repair-taxonomy.md), not a second menu.

> **Plain-language version.** EPAs are the real jobs a new doctor has to be trusted to do; WBA is a
> supervisor *watching them do it* on a real patient and writing it down. Because someone actually
> observed the doing, this kind of evidence is unusually trustworthy in the AI era — you can't fake a
> bedside encounter with a chatbot. The two places it *can* go wrong are (a) too few observations to
> generalize, and (b) one observation being treated as a pass/fail entrustment decision on its own. The
> fix for both is the same UME instinct: **aggregate many low-stakes observations and let a committee
> decide.**

---

## 1. What EPAs and WBA are

### Entrustable Professional Activities (EPAs)

An **EPA** is a discrete, observable **unit of professional practice** — a whole task a clinician does —
that can be **entrusted** to a trainee once they are competent to do it with a given level of
supervision. EPAs are deliberately *holistic*: a single EPA integrates multiple competencies (knowledge,
skills, communication, professionalism) into one real-world activity, rather than assessing each
competency in isolation. The unit of judgment is "can we trust this learner to *do this task*?", not "did
they score X% on a knowledge test."

The **AAMC Core EPAs for Entering Residency are 13** activities every graduating medical student is
expected to be able to perform — **with appropriate supervision** — on day one of residency, **regardless
of specialty**. They define a shared floor for the UME-to-GME transition.

| # | Core EPA (entering residency) |
|----|-------------------------------|
| 1 | Gather a history and perform a physical examination. |
| 2 | Prioritize a differential diagnosis following a clinical encounter. |
| 3 | Recommend and interpret common diagnostic and screening tests. |
| 4 | Enter and discuss orders and prescriptions. |
| 5 | Document a clinical encounter in the patient record. |
| 6 | Provide an oral presentation of a clinical encounter. |
| 7 | Form clinical questions and retrieve evidence to advance patient care. |
| 8 | Give or receive a patient handover to transition care responsibility. |
| 9 | Collaborate as a member of an interprofessional team. |
| 10 | Recognize a patient requiring urgent or emergent care and initiate evaluation and management. |
| 11 | Obtain informed consent for tests and/or procedures. |
| 12 | Perform the general procedures of a physician. |
| 13 | Identify system failures and contribute to a culture of safety and improvement. |

> Treat this list as authoritative for *what an EPA looks like*, not as a curriculum mandate — schools
> vary in how many they assess and when. For institution-specific framing and any local EPA set, see
> [`domain-pack.md`](domain-pack.md).

**A few worked examples of how an EPA frames a learning claim:**
- **EPA 1 (history + physical):** the claim is *"can perform a focused, accurate H&P on a real patient,"*
  not *"can list the components of a history."* The evidence has to be the *doing*.
- **EPA 2 (prioritize a differential):** the claim is *"can build and prioritize a differential after an
  actual encounter,"* i.e., clinical reasoning in context — not an after-the-fact written rationale.
- **EPA 10 (recognize urgent/emergent care):** the claim is *"can recognize the sick patient and start
  management,"* a judgment-in-the-moment that a polished write-up cannot demonstrate.
- **EPA 12 (general procedures):** the claim is a *performed* procedural skill, observed.

### The entrustment / supervision scale

Entrustment is expressed as **how much supervision the trainee still needs** — the scale *is* the
score. A common ordinal progression:

| Level | What it means |
|-------|----------------|
| **Direct supervision** | Supervisor physically present, observing and co-acting. |
| **Indirect supervision** | Supervisor available (in the building / on call) but not in the room. |
| **Independent practice** | Trainee performs the activity unsupervised. |
| **(May supervise others)** | Trainee is trusted enough to oversee more junior learners. |

The key property for our purposes: an **entrustment decision is an aggregate judgment**, built from
**many observations over time**, never a single look. A supervisor's rating on one encounter is a *data
point*, not the decision.

### How WBA feeds entrustment

**Workplace-based assessment (WBA)** is **direct observation of authentic performance in the clinical
workplace** — the trainee doing the real activity with real patients and teams. Common instruments:

- **Mini-CEX** (mini-Clinical Evaluation Exercise) — a brief observed clinical encounter.
- **Direct Observation of Procedural Skills (DOPS)** and other direct-observation forms.
- **Entrustment/supervision-level ratings** captured after an observed activity (often app-based).
- Multisource feedback and observed handovers (e.g., EPA 8).

WBA is **primarily formative**: each instance is a low-stakes snapshot whose main job is to give feedback
*and* contribute a data point. Those data points **accumulate** and are reviewed — typically by a
**competence / clinical-competency committee** — to support an **entrustment decision**. This is
**programmatic assessment** in action: the high-stakes decision is decoupled from any one instrument and
made from the *pattern* (see [`../../core/repair-taxonomy.md`](../../core/repair-taxonomy.md) §3.4).

> **Miller's pyramid placement.** EPAs/WBA target the **"Does"** apex — performance in real practice —
> the top of *Knows → Knows How → Shows How → Does*
> ([`../../core/repair-taxonomy.md`](../../core/repair-taxonomy.md) §3.3). That is exactly why this
> evidence is valuable, and exactly why it is comparatively robust to AI.

## 2. Where EPAs/WBA sit on the chain

Run the same five links from the keystone. The pattern below is the **default** for *genuinely observed*
WBA; always read each link at the **case's actual stakes** (the keystone's one severity knob), because
entrustment decisions are often consequential.

| Link | Default state for observed WBA | Why |
|------|-------------------------------|-----|
| **1 · Construct** | Usually **Intact** | The EPA names the construct at an assessable, authentic grain (a real professional activity). The risk is mismatch — see caution below. |
| **2 · Scoring** | **Strong** | Performance is *observed* and *attributable on its face* — a rater watched **this** trainee do it. This is WBA's headline strength in the AI era. *(Assumes in-person, physically observed doing; talk-only or remote-observed performance is not automatically "unaided" — read for covert real-time AI as a watch-out, per keystone Edge cases.)* |
| **3 · Generalization** | **Common risk** | One mini-CEX, one rater, one case rarely characterizes the trainee's range across patients/contexts/occasions. |
| **4 · Extrapolation** | **Strong** | The task *is* the authentic "Does" activity — transfer to the real target is near-direct, not inferred from a proxy artifact. |
| **5 · Implications** | **Common risk** | The danger is letting a **single observation drive an entrustment decision**. One look can't carry a consequential progression/entrustment gate. |

**Read this as the chain intends:** observed WBA is *strong exactly where unsupervised written work is
weak* (Scoring + Extrapolation). So when WBA breaks, it almost always breaks **downstream** — at
**Generalization** (too thin) or **Implications** (decision exceeds the evidence) — not at attribution.

### The two characteristic breaks

**Generalization (Link 3) — too few observations.** A handful of encounters, or all from one rotation /
one rater / one patient type, can't support a domain-wide competence claim. *Cause test (keystone): is
the evidence base itself too thin to describe the trainee's range, independent of any decision?* If yes,
it's Generalization. **Repair direction:** sample across more cases, contexts, occasions, and raters —
*repeated low-stakes observations* and *triangulation*
([`../../core/repair-taxonomy.md`](../../core/repair-taxonomy.md) §2.3).

**Implications (Link 5) — one observation as the decision.** The sampling may even be adequate, but a
**single instrument or single occasion is being treated as the entrustment verdict**. This is the UME
form of the keystone's **Example 6** (a sound OSCE used as the *sole* progression gate): the instrument
is fine; the *use* exceeds it. **Repair direction:** aggregate across the programme and route the
decision to a **competence committee** — *apply programmatic-assessment principles*, *triangulate*,
*require qualified human review* ([`../../core/repair-taxonomy.md`](../../core/repair-taxonomy.md) §2.5,
§3.4). Because entrustment is **consequential**, this also trips the **Needs-deeper-review override**
(keystone Phase D): the headline becomes "this needs the committee," with the chain reported as a note.

> **Disambiguation (straight from the keystone).** *Too-thin-to-describe-range* = **Generalization**.
> *Adequate-for-feedback-but-not-for-this-consequence* = **Implications**. Test the **cause**, not the
> symptom (keystone, Link 5 note + Example 6).

### Construct caution (when WBA is *not* automatically fine)

Link 1 is usually intact, but check two UME-specific mismatches before declaring it so:
- **Documentation standing in for the activity.** If the "WBA" is actually a *written note about* the
  encounter rather than the *observed* encounter (e.g., grading EPA 5 documentation as a proxy for the
  whole clinical activity), the construct may be under-represented — and that written artifact is now
  AI-exposable (see §3). That is a **Construct** or **Scoring** concern, not the robust observed case.
- **Rater drift / unanchored entrustment.** If entrustment ratings reward general impression or
  likeability rather than the EPA's defined behaviors, the score carries construct-irrelevant variance.
  That is a Construct (rubric) or Scoring (rater) issue — repair with rater training / anchored scales
  ([`../../core/repair-taxonomy.md`](../../core/repair-taxonomy.md) §2.1, §2.2), not more observations.

## 3. AI-era considerations for WBA/EPAs

The headline: **observed clinical performance is robust to AI.** A trainee cannot have a chatbot take a
history at the bedside, run the code, or perform the procedure. The *observed "Does"* evidence is exactly
the kind the rest of this skill spends its energy trying to recover for written work — WBA already has it.
This is a **restraint signal**, not a redesign trigger (see §4).

But EPAs/WBA still carry **written and self-reported components**, and *those* inherit the ordinary
AI-era Scoring exposure:

| WBA component | AI exposure | What to watch |
|---------------|-------------|----------------|
| **The observed encounter itself** | Robust | Performance is live and attributable — no change needed. |
| **Written WBA documentation / clinical notes** | Exposable | An AI-drafted note doesn't prove the trainee *did* or *reasoned through* the encounter. Score the **observation**, not the prose. |
| **Narrative supervisor feedback** | Exposable (if AI-assisted) | AI-generated narrative is fine as a *writing aid* but must reflect a real observation — never let it **substitute for** observing. |
| **Trainee self-assessment / reflection** | Exposable | A fluent reflection can be AI-produced; tie it to a *specific observed behavior* (keystone Link 4 repair). |

**Three hard lines (cross-reference, do not duplicate):**

1. **Never let an AI-generated narrative substitute for observation.** The entrustment signal is the
   *watching*; AI may help write it up, but a write-up without an underlying observation is not WBA.
2. **No entrustment (or any consequential UME decision) on AI output alone.** This is a binary safety
   gate — see [`ume-governance-guardrails.md`](ume-governance-guardrails.md) and
   [`../../core/safety-guardrails.md`](../../core/safety-guardrails.md) (Gate 3). AI-involved evaluation
   requires **qualified faculty/committee review**.
3. **Watch the Scoring of the *written* components specifically** — not the observed performance. If an
   AI worry shows up here, it is a Scoring break on the *documentation*, repaired by anchoring the score
   to the observation (and, if needed, an oral/observed check), **not** by abandoning WBA.

> Equity note: if WBA documentation or reflection is moved into a tooling workflow that assumes app /
> device / connectivity access, raise the standard access flag
> ([`../../core/equity-and-access-flags.md`](../../core/equity-and-access-flags.md)) — but the *observed*
> core is unaffected.

## 4. Coaching guidance

### Use WBA/EPAs to repair an **Extrapolation** break ("move to *Does*")

When the primary diagnosis upstream is **Extrapolation** — a polished artifact that doesn't show the
trainee can *do* the clinical thing — the canonical repair is to **move the evidence up Miller's pyramid
toward "Does"** ([`../../core/repair-taxonomy.md`](../../core/repair-taxonomy.md) §2.4, §3.3). In UME
that move *is* WBA/EPA evidence: replace or augment the write-up with an **observed encounter** mapped to
the relevant EPA (e.g., an observed H&P for EPA 1; an observed differential-in-context for EPA 2; an
observed handover for EPA 8). Recommend this when:
- the claim is about *doing/reasoning in context* but the evidence is a written product, **and**
- the target is genuinely clinical performance (don't over-climb when *Knows How* is the real target —
  keystone UME caution; §3.3).

### Use programmatic aggregation to repair an **Implications** break

When the break is **Implications** — a consequential entrustment/progression decision resting on **one**
observation or instrument — the repair is **not** "observe harder," it is **aggregate**: pull together
many low-stakes WBA data points across raters/contexts and route the decision to a **competence
committee** ([`../../core/repair-taxonomy.md`](../../core/repair-taxonomy.md) §2.5, §3.4;
[`ume-governance-guardrails.md`](ume-governance-guardrails.md)). Decouple the high-stakes call from any
single instrument. Flag the case **Needs deeper review** (consequential stakes → keystone Phase D
override) and report the chain as a note for the committee.

### Restraint — observed WBA is often *already strong*

This is the most important coaching instinct for this file. A **genuinely observed, multi-occasion,
committee-reviewed** EPA/WBA programme frequently has **no broken link**: Construct intact (authentic
activity), Scoring strong (observed/attributable), Generalization intact (sampled across encounters),
Extrapolation strong (the real "Does"), Implications intact (programmatic, committee-owned). The correct
output is then the **Strong / restraint** variant
([`../../core/response-pattern.md`](../../core/response-pattern.md)) — acknowledge the strength, suggest
at most one lightweight clarification, and **do not manufacture a redesign**. Forcing AI-resilience work
onto an already-robust observed assessment is itself a failure mode (Gate 5 restraint;
[`../../core/safety-guardrails.md`](../../core/safety-guardrails.md)).

> **Quick decision aid.**
> - Worried about *attribution*? With observed WBA, you usually shouldn't be — Scoring is strong. Look
>   downstream.
> - *Too few looks?* → **Generalization** → sample more (cases / contexts / occasions / raters).
> - *One look driving the decision?* → **Implications** → aggregate + committee (programmatic).
> - *AI worry?* → it lives in the **written** components → score the observation, not the prose.
> - *Nothing broken?* → say so. Restraint is the answer.
