## The faculty message

> Hi — I direct the pre-clerkship [PLACEHOLDER: renal/cardio] foundations block at **[PLACEHOLDER:
> institution]**. Our main summative is a closed-book, in-person, proctored exam: a blueprinted set of
> single-best-answer items across the block's mechanisms and pharmacology — pathophysiology,
> drug mechanisms, the basic-to-clinical links. Students sit it in a monitored hall, no devices, no
> notes.
>
> Honestly I'm getting nervous reading all the AI-and-assessment guidance flying around. Everyone says
> we have to redesign everything for the AI era. Should I be building an AI component into this exam, or
> adding some kind of AI-collaboration task so it's not "behind the times"? I don't want to be the
> dinosaur who got caught out.

## The coach reply

## My quick read
This one holds up. It's a secure, proctored, blueprinted foundational-knowledge exam, and the thing
you're certifying — that the student *knows and can apply the mechanisms* on their own — is exactly the
thing this format is built to show. Under likely AI use the chain doesn't break anywhere, so the honest
answer is the one that's hardest to hear amid all the "redesign everything" noise: **no major redesign
needed, and you should not bolt AI onto this.**

Learning signal: the student can recall and apply the block's foundational science — mechanism,
pathophysiology, pharmacology — independently.

Readiness: Strong.

## Why it holds
Walk it and every link stays intact under likely AI use. The construct is well-defined and the items
elicit it. Attribution is sound — it's proctored and in-person, so there's no unsupervised artifact for
AI to ghost-write; the student does the work in the room. A blueprinted item set samples across the
block's topics rather than betting on one item. For a *Knows / Knows How* claim, a written
single-best-answer exam is the right Miller level — you're not over-reaching toward bedside performance.
And the stakes (a block summative) are matched to the evidence and human-overseen. Nothing here is
AI-vulnerable, so there's nothing to repair.

A direct answer to your worry: **"No AI" is a legitimate, often-correct design here — not a failure to
modernize.** When the construct *is* independent recall and synthesis, a secure closed-book exam is valid
*because* it keeps AI out. Forcing an AI-collaboration task onto it wouldn't make it more rigorous; it
would change what you're measuring — quietly turning "knows the mechanism" into "can work with a tool" —
and weaken a signal that's currently clean. That's the trap to avoid, not the one you're in.

## Optional polish (only if you want it)
At most, add **one line** to the exam instructions making the no-device / no-AI condition explicit for
any out-of-room prep (a sentence, not a redesign). If your instructions already say this, **nothing
needed** — leave it alone.

## Want help drafting?
If it's useful, I can draft that single line of AI-use clarity for the exam instructions. If you later
have a *different* assessment in this block where the claim is about clinical reasoning or doing — not
recall — that's where an AI-era redesign conversation actually belongs, and I'm glad to take that one up
separately.

## How the coach got there — the five-link walk

> Read at the case's **actual stakes** (a block summative — matched, not "consequential": a normal
> summative course exam does **not** trip the override), under the **likely** AI-use condition (none in
> the room — proctored, no devices, so the exam is not AI-vulnerable). The walk runs to completion
> because **no link is Severed**.

| # | Link | State | Why |
|---|------|-------|-----|
| 1 | **Construct** | **Intact** | The claim (independent recall/application of foundational science) is well-defined, and blueprinted single-best-answer items genuinely elicit it. |
| 2 | **Scoring** | **Intact** | Proctored, in-person, no devices → attributable on its face; scored against a key. No unsupervised artifact for AI to produce; no rater-attribution gap. |
| 3 | **Generalization** | **Intact** | A blueprinted set samples across the block's topics — not one narrow item over-read as broad competence. |
| 4 | **Extrapolation** | **Intact** | For a *Knows / Knows How* claim, a written exam is the right Miller level. The certified target *is* foundational knowledge — no transfer over-reach to bedside doing. |
| 5 | **Implications** | **Intact** | Block-summative stakes are matched to the evidence and human-overseen. Not consequential (no progression/competency/licensing gate) → **no Needs-deeper-review override**. |

**Primary diagnosis:** none — the chain holds under likely AI use.
**Secondary findings:** none.
**Readiness:** Strong (all links Intact).

**Why restraint is the correct, tested output.** When no link breaks, the coach **stops** — it does not
invent a redesign, and it does **not** force AI integration into a secure, closed-book, knowledge-anchored
exam whose construct *is* independent recall/synthesis. Manufacturing a problem here (or pushing an
AI-collaboration task onto a valid "No AI" design) is itself a failure mode — the restraint gate. The
most the coach offers is one lightweight clarification (a single line of AI-use language for out-of-room
prep), or "nothing needed." The AI-era redesign conversation belongs on a *different* assessment — one
whose claim is about reasoning or doing, not recall.
