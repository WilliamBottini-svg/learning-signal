# Engine Red-Team — Learning Signal Check (2026-06-06)

> **Model-based adversarial review, NOT human-expert ratification.** Same model family wrote the
> engine and ran this red-team, so this cannot certify the engine — it surfaces issues and proposes
> resolutions to triage. **Snapshot at adjudication (2026-06-06):** `core/` had not yet received v0.2
> seam fixes. **Post-release status:** see [`engine-redteam-remediation-status.md`](engine-redteam-remediation-status.md).
> Human review (M7) still required.

## Method

Two adversarial lenses (a hostile validity theorist; a skeptical front-line educator) attacked five
clusters of the engine; raw findings were de-duplicated; each was then **steelman-defended** and
**adjudicated** by a neutral agent (verdict + severity + proposed fix); two completeness critics then
hunted for what was missed. 63 agents total.

- Raw findings: 60 → deduped/adjudicated: **25** · completeness-critic adds: **19** · combined: **44**
- Adjudicated verdicts: **19 real-issue**, **6 document-limitation**
- Adjudicated severity: 1 High · 21 Medium · 3 Low  |  Critic severity: 6 High · 11 Medium · 2 Low

**Bottom line:** no architectural collapse — the ordered-walk keystone survives scrutiny. But there
are real, fixable issues, mostly **Medium**, concentrated in (a) safety/control-flow *seams* between
the engine and the reception layer, (b) under-specified link boundaries that threaten the
"same-primary-diagnosis" promise, (c) **scholarly attribution** (Kane vs Messick), and (d) **coverage**
of group / AI-integrated / formative / portfolio / low-resource cases. The single most important
theme: **the engine's safety guarantees are weaker than advertised at the seams** (PHI re-entry via
triage/delivery; "assume low stakes" fail-open; equity flag droppable; restraint has no false-clear guard).

## Headline issues (fix-first candidates)

- **[B3 · High]** The 'consequential' override boundary has no operational rule for the modal case (a grade that *by itself* triggers a listed decision), so two readers route the same artifact to opposite response shapes.
- **[Critic#10 · High]** Phase 0 screens only the intake, but the triage 'summarize back' and the 'paste-ready artifact' delivery steps re-open the door to the PHI/records Gate 1 exists to keep out — the highest-severity leak path, via two correct-in-isolation instructions.
- **[Critic#11 · High]** The missing-intake rule says 'assume low stakes and proceed,' but stakes is the consequential-override trigger — converting a fail-safe (consequential→human review) into fail-open on the most common condition (an under-specified message).
- **[Critic#1 · High]** The intake protocol (one question max, never consecutive) forces the coach to self-supply the very inputs (stakes, likely AI-use) that determine the diagnosis, then emit a categorical readiness with no confidence qualifier — 'mechanical, never by vibe' becomes 'mechanical from values the coach invented.'
- **[Critic#12 · High]** The equity/access flag lives only in Watch-outs, which the terse 'just tell me' variant drops entirely — so the engine can emit an access-dependent repair while silently dropping the access flag the equity file calls mandatory.
- **[Critic#5 · High]** Restraint guards only *over*-prescription (Gate 5); there is no symmetric guard against *false restraint* (clearing a broken assessment as Strong), and three mechanisms (charitable boundary tests, the multiplicative-zero rule, assume-low-stakes) all push toward Strong.
- **[Critic#2 · High]** 'Proctored / observed' is treated as canonical attribution and exemplar 'Strong,' but the docs never model that supervision is itself AI-vulnerable (earpiece/second-screen/rehearsed AI prep) — risk of over-rating secure formats.
- **[C2-1 · Medium]** Kane fidelity: the keystone claims to 'operationalize Kane' but prepends a 'Construct' link (Messick's CIV/underrepresentation) — Kane's chain has no Construct inference. A validity scholar would reject the attribution; fix the framing, keep the chain.
- **[A1 · Medium]** 'Repair the upstream link first' conflates Kane's logical ordering with repair sequencing; for *orthogonal* co-breaks the upstream fix doesn't resolve the downstream break, yet Phase E only optimizes the primary link — an educator can ship a half-fixed assessment believing it's whole.
- **[A2 · Medium]** The 'another agent reaches the same primary diagnosis' promise is contradicted by the engine's own 'genuinely ambiguous cases' and the un-operationalized Severed-vs-Weakened cut; reword to the honest conditional form.

> Note the convergence with the prior eval's A03 finding: **Critic#10** is the general form of the
> Gate-1 PHI-echo gap already patched — the sensitive-data boundary leaks wherever the reply restates
> input, and triage/delivery were never interlocked with Gate 1.

## Adjudicated findings (by cluster)

### A — Structural / logical

#### A1 — A-structural · **Medium** · real-issue

- **Claim:** The 'repair the primary (upstream) link first' mandate is presented as logically necessary, but it conflates two different orderings: (a) the LOGICAL order in which Kane's inferences presuppose each other, and (b) the PRACTICAL order in which repairs should be sequenced. A higher link being unwarranted makes the overall interpretive argument unsound — it does NOT make a lower link's warrant moot for repair purposes. For genuine co-breaks the upstream repair often does NOT resolve, and is not even a prerequisite to, an independent downstream break; the doc silently inverts a true negative claim ('attribution can't fix the wrong construct') into a false positive workflow claim ('fix the upstream link and the downstream concern can wait'). When the cheapest, highest-utility, most decision-relevant move targets a DOWNSTREAM link, the upstream-first rule forces the more expensive, two-step path and explicitly forbids leading with the downstream fix as 'a sophisticated downstream repair [that] distract[s] from an upstream break' — exactly the disproportionate move Phase E claims to prevent (its 'lightest repair' optimization is scoped only to the primary link, so a light downstream fix is never compared against a heavy upstream one).
- **Where:** core/diagnostic-procedure.md lines 8-11, 18-21, 185-186, 208-209, 241, 260, 305-321, 352-364; core/skill-core.md line 41
- **Attack:** Counterexamples the doc supplies against itself: (1) Phase C lines 317-320 — a take-home clinical-reasoning essay, Primary=Scoring, Secondary=Extrapolation: fixing Scoring (pre-AI commitment, oral probe) confirms the student WROTE it but does nothing for the orthogonal Extrapolation break; an educator who follows the single 'first repair' walks away believing the assessment is fixed when its transfer inference is still severed. (2) Example 4 (group slide deck): the prescribed Scoring fix (individual reflection) does not make an individual grade proportionate to thin individual evidence; the doc concedes 'then consider lowering the stakes' as an afterthought, admitting the upstream fix did not resolve the downstream break. (3) A moderate-stakes essay with Construct Weakened (rubric over-weights polish) AND Implications Severed (sole gate): the chain prescribes a weeks-long rubric/task rebuild, when the cheapest, most decision-relevant fix is the downstream one-line policy change (stop using one essay as the sole gate; triangulate). 'Earliest, not cheapest or most harmful' is the wrong sort order for a tool that markets proportionality.
- **Verdict (real-issue, Medium):** The doc silently converts a true logical claim (an unsound upstream link makes the whole interpretation unsound) into a false workflow claim (fix upstream first and the downstream can wait), and Phase E's proportionality check is scoped to the primary link only, so for orthogonal co-breaks (including the doc's own Phase C essay example) it can call an assessment fixed when a downstream link is still severed and never lets a cheap downstream fix compete with a heavy upstream one, contradicting the tool's proportionality thesis.
- **Proposed fix (advisory):** Make three bounded edits, all in core/diagnostic-procedure.md (with the mirror in core/skill-core.md lines 37-41). (1) Split the conflated claim at lines 9-11 / contract item 4 (lines 20-21): keep "the chain is only as strong as its earliest broken link" (true of the validity argument and the diagnosis-naming/headline rule, which should stay) but stop asserting that presupposition entails repair-first. State explicitly that the upstream break is always the named PRIMARY DIAGNOSIS, while repair sequencing depends on whether co-breaks are nested or orthogonal. (2) Add the missing branch in Phase C (around lines 315-320): define nested co-breaks (downstream is a shadow of the upstream and dissolves when it is repaired -> fix upstream first) vs orthogonal co-breaks (independent; the upstream repair will not touch the downstream break). Add the boundary test, parallel to the existing Construct-vs-Scoring and Generalization-vs-Implications tests: "Does repairing the upstream link change the downstream link's state? Yes -> nested. No -> orthogonal." Fix the Phase C worked example itself (lines 317-320): it is orthogonal (Scoring fix does nothing for Extrapolation, as the doc already concedes), so the output must say the attribution fix does NOT resolve the still-severed transfer link, not merely defer it to "a later repair." (3) Widen Phase E's optimization scope (lines 358-364): change "the lightest repair that restores the broken link" so that, for orthogonal co-breaks, the lightest decision-relevant repair is compared ACROSS all broken links (not only the primary), and require the emitted output to state that the upstream fix leaves the downstream break open so no user reads a half-fixed assessment as whole. Keep Phase F's single-headline / one-next-move discipline, but allow the one next move to be the highest-utility repair across links when breaks are orthogonal. Optionally add one golden/adversarial test case for an orthogonal co-break to lock the behavior.

#### A2 — A-structural · **Medium** · real-issue

- **Claim:** The keystone determinism promise — 'Another agent (or model) following this file on the same case should arrive at the same primary diagnosis' (lines 4-5) and 'Readiness is derived from the walk, never assigned by vibe / follows mechanically' (lines 326, 478-479) — is structurally impossible to honor, because the primary diagnosis (the EARLIEST Severed link) is fully determined only after fixing three irreducibly subjective inputs the doc never operationalizes: (1) the 'likely real AI-use behavior' (line 90, explicitly 'often differ[s]' and is guessed when unstated, lines 100-104); (2) the assigned STAKES tier (line 91), which the doc concedes is 'the single severity knob' that can flip a link from Intact to Severed (lines 158-162) and is itself fuzzy; (3) the Intact/Weakened/Severed call, which hinges on undefined words ('could plausibly earn the same result', 'mostly reflects the student's own work'). Because the walk HALTS at the earliest break, a single disagreement on any one input changes the entire primary diagnosis and recommended repair — the ordered-halt design AMPLIFIES rater disagreement rather than averaging it out. The doc elsewhere celebrates this variability ('the same artifact can be Moderate at low stakes and Vulnerable at high stakes... That is intended', lines 161-162; 'Stakes-monotonicity is expected', lines 346-348), directly contradicting the line-5 reproducibility promise.
- **Where:** core/diagnostic-procedure.md lines 4-5, 90-91, 100-104, 149-162, 305-311, 324-348, 478-479; core/skill-core.md lines 60-68
- **Attack:** The engine's own golden-test file disproves the determinism claim. tests/golden-test-cases.md lines 391-426 ('Genuinely ambiguous cases') concedes: G15 — 'a reasonable expert could instead score the first response as the missing-intake variant... Both are defensible'; G09 — uncertainty over whether expected readiness is Vulnerable vs Moderate; G07 vs G11 — 'an expert could argue the case has co-broken Construct and Scoring and debate which to call primary,' and 'a base model frequently fixes these by proctoring (the Scoring move),' i.e. real agents following the file land on a DIFFERENT primary link. Concretely, an educator submitting a take-home reflective essay that 'counts toward the grade': Agent A reads stakes as moderate and AI-use as 'students mostly write their own' → Construct/Scoring Weakened → Moderate; Agent B reads 'counts toward a pass/fail line' as consequential and assumes worst-case AI use → Construct Severed → override. Same case, different readiness tier and repair urgency, both defensible under the file. A reproducibility claim contradicted by the author's own regression suite is an overclaim.
- **Verdict (real-issue, Medium):** The attack's "structurally impossible" framing overreaches (stakes-monotonicity is f(artifact,stakes), not non-determinism, and the worked counterexample requires both agents to violate the documented intake rule), but it correctly exposes an unconditional line-5 promise the author's own golden suite contradicts (G07/G11, G09, G15) plus the one genuinely un-operationalized decision — Severed-vs-Weakened — that the halt propagates straight to the primary diagnosis and repair urgency.
- **Proposed fix (advisory):** Make two narrow, additive changes (do NOT abandon the keystone). (1) Reword the unconditional line 4-5 promise to its honest conditional form, e.g.: "Another agent following this file should arrive at the same primary diagnosis WHEN the case inputs (stakes, likely AI-use, evidence type) are specified; where an input is under-specified, faithful agents should converge on the same clarifying question rather than guess (see Phase A, lines 99-104)." This removes the attack surface at zero cost and aligns line 5 with the author's own ambiguous-cases section. (2) Add a "Severed vs Weakened — boundary test" callout in Phase B alongside the two existing which-link tests (Construct-vs-Scoring at 214-225; Generalization-vs-Implications at 290-301), giving an operational cue for the cut at lines 151-156 — e.g., anchor "could plausibly earn the same result" to a concrete bar (a non-learner reaches the SAME score/pass-band, not merely a higher one) and tie the Weakened ceiling to "a one-line/low-burden fix restores it." Optionally cross-reference G09 as the canonical Severed-vs-Weakened example. No change to the ordered-halt architecture is warranted.

#### A3 — A-structural · **Low** · document-limitation

- **Claim:** The 'halt at the earliest broken link' rule is justified by a measurement-theoretic asymmetry ('No amount of attribution or sampling fixes measuring the wrong thing', line 186; 'Do not optimize downstream links while you cannot attribute the evidence to the student', line 209) that only holds pairwise for Construct→Scoring and is then over-generalized to all five links as if it were a theorem. The asymmetry does NOT hold for most pairs — e.g., a Scoring break (attribution) and a Generalization break (under-sampling) are largely orthogonal, and the cheapest single move can fix both at once. By halting at the earliest break and refusing to consider whether a downstream repair is a superset/dominating move, the procedure can recommend a strictly dominated repair plan. The 'halt' is a diagnostic triage convenience dressed up as a necessity; Kane's chain concerns the validity of the ARGUMENT, not a forced repair sequence.
- **Where:** core/diagnostic-procedure.md lines 18-22, 185-186, 208-209, 241, 260, 305-312
- **Attack:** A course uses ONE unsupervised take-home essay as its entire grade. The walk halts at Scoring (Severed, unattributable); the doc's first repair is a proctored/oral attribution check on that one essay. But the binding problem is also Generalization (one artifact for a whole-course claim). A single move — replace the one essay with a sequence of three short in-class checks — repairs Scoring AND Generalization at once and is cheaper than proctoring the essay and later adding more samples. The halt-at-Scoring rule blocks the agent from seeing the dominating combined fix, because it forbids 'optimizing downstream links' before the upstream one is repaired.
- **Verdict (document-limitation, Low):** The attack's measurement-theory critique is correct and the defense concedes it (the asymmetry is over-generalized in the prose), but the structural halt is sound and the alleged dominated-repair-plan is not actually mandated — so the docs should calibrate the justification and add an explicit superset-repair clause rather than change the design.
- **Proposed fix (advisory):** Keep the ordered halt unchanged — it is sound and load-bearing — but tighten two pieces of prose. (1) Calibrate the per-link justification language in core/diagnostic-procedure.md: the strong measurement-theoretic claim ("no amount of attribution/sampling fixes measuring the wrong thing", line 186) is genuinely earned only at Construct (Link 1). For Scoring->downstream (lines 208-209, and the identical "Halt." stamps at lines 241, 260), reword to the accurate interpretive basis: the downstream claim is *uninterpretable* until the upstream link holds (you cannot characterize "the student's range" when you cannot establish the student did the work), so name the upstream break as the headline first. Avoid implying a uniform repair-theoretic theorem the lower links do not satisfy. (2) Add one explicit clause to Phase C (around line 312-320) and cross-reference it from Phase E (line 358-359): a single repair instrument that restores the primary link AND a secondary one is *preferred*, and choosing it does not violate the ordering — the halt orders which break is the *headline* and which link is repaired *first*, not which *instrument* is used. Make clear (echoing repair-taxonomy.md lines 25/27, which currently read as strictly "sequence secondary repairs later") that a combined/superset move is the staged-repairs ideal, not an exception to it. Optionally add a worked example where one move (e.g., replacing one take-home essay with a short sequence of in-class checks) restores Scoring and Generalization together, paralleling Example 4's sequential group-work case.

### B — Definitional / boundary

#### B3 — B-boundary · **High** · real-issue

- **Claim:** The 'consequential' override boundary — the single most outcome-determining input, since it flips the entire response SHAPE to 'Needs deeper review' (line 343) — is drawn by an enumerated list (progression, promotion, remediation, competency, licensing, disciplinary; line 91) while line 338 carves out 'a normal summative course grade' as NOT automatically consequential, with no operational rule for the dominant in-between cases. In any pass/fail or GPA-gated program a summative grade IS an input to exactly the listed consequential decisions (a failing grade triggers remediation; a GPA gates progression/scholarships/licensure eligibility), so the bright line and the consequential list overlap in practice and the doc resolves the overlap circularly: a grade is non-consequential until it causes a consequence the doc itself calls consequential. The fuzzy boundary propagates into TWO determinations (the Severed/Weakened threshold via 'stakes lower the bar', and the override trigger), and 'high-stakes exams' (line 333) vs 'not automatically consequential summative grade' (line 338) gives no rule for a high-weight final. 'Stakes-monotonicity is expected' (lines 346-348) is invoked to immunize this but only relabels the inconsistency as intended.
- **Where:** core/diagnostic-procedure.md lines 91, 158-162, 333-348; core/safety-guardrails.md Gate 3 scope note lines 120-124; Example 4 line 441; tests/golden-test-cases.md lines 138-139
- **Attack:** Course directors A and B paste the identical unsupervised take-home final — the only graded summative in a required pre-clerkship course; failing it fails the course, and a course failure automatically convenes a promotions-committee review (and, for an international student, can mean visa loss). A reads 'summative course grade → not automatically consequential' (line 338) and rates Vulnerable with a light proctoring fix; B reads 'a failure feeds remediation/progression → consequential' and rates Needs deeper review, routing to governance. Same artifact, opposite outputs and opposite response shapes, both defensible under the text. The carve-out is simultaneously over-inclusive (a formative-feedback OSCE that contributes one data point to an eventual progression committee gets dragged to 'Needs deeper review' because the word 'progression' appears) and under-inclusive (real failure/scholarship/prerequisite gates fall through the gap). The 'mechanical' readiness derivation collapses precisely on the input the doc calls 'the single severity knob.'
- **Verdict (real-issue, High):** The override boundary lacks any rule for the modal transmission case — a grade that by itself deterministically triggers a listed decision — so two readers reach opposite response SHAPES on the identical artifact, both textually defensible, and monotonicity addresses only the continuous severity slide, not this binary classification flip.
- **Proposed fix (advisory):** Add one operational sufficiency/causation rule to the consequential boundary, co-located at the three places it governs: core/diagnostic-procedure.md line 91 (Stakes definition) and lines 335-339 (the override), and core/safety-guardrails.md Gate 3 scope note (lines 121-124). Proposed text: "A grade is consequential when failing it (or the GPA/credit it feeds) BY ITSELF — without further independent human judgment — triggers one of the listed decisions (progression, promotion, remediation, competency, licensing, disciplinary); i.e., the grade is a sufficient cause of that decision. A grade that is merely one input among many to a later committee judgment is NOT, by itself, consequential." Then add a golden test case for the modal transmission configuration (a pass/fail course final whose failure auto-convenes a promotions/progression review) expected to route to Needs deeper review, sitting between G03 (high-weight, gates nothing → Strong) and G19 (sole gate → override). Optionally rephrase the brittle keyword exposure so a formative artifact that merely "feeds one data point" into an eventual committee is explicitly excluded.

#### B1 — B-boundary · **Medium** · real-issue

- **Claim:** The Construct-vs-Scoring boundary test ('if a student did the intended thinking honestly, would this task capture it? Yes → Scoring; No → Construct', lines 214-225) is circular and under-determined, and it returns the OPPOSITE answer to the doc's own worked classifications. It conditions on 'did the intended thinking honestly' — i.e. it assumes away attribution and AI use, the very thing the tool exists to analyze — so almost any task passes the 'Yes' test trivially, collapsing nearly everything into Scoring and starving Construct. Worse, the test contradicts the CIV rule in the same file: a rubric that pays out for fluency/length/polish (the doc's own CIV example, lines 180-182) is STILL captured 'if a student did the intended thinking honestly' (an honest, thoughtful student also writes fluent, long, polished prose), so the boundary test answers 'Scoring,' flatly contradicting line 222 which insists the same rubric 'fails here [Construct].' The test also tensions with lines 141-143 ('AI exposes a pre-existing construct underrepresentation problem... that is a Construct break, not a Scoring break'): the same unverifiable take-home write-up is sorted to Scoring by the boundary test but to Construct by the pre-existing-weakness rule, and because the chain halts at the earliest break this contradiction changes the entire diagnosis and first repair (proctor vs redesign). The 'honest student' framing and the 'AI-suppliable surface features' framing are two different tests bolted together, and they disagree.
- **Where:** core/diagnostic-procedure.md lines 141-143, 168-189, 214-225, 386-388; tests/golden-test-cases.md lines 414-421
- **Attack:** A discussion-board / peer-feedback task graded on a rubric (e.g. 25% thoroughness/length, 25% clarity/polish, 50% cites readings; AI supplies all four cheaply). Apply the boundary test literally: an honest student writing a long, clear, well-cited post scores full marks → 'Yes → Scoring.' But the doc's CIV rule labels the same fluency-rewarding rubric a Construct break. The engine's own author-flag (golden-test-cases.md lines 414-421, G07/G11) admits these are co-broken Construct AND Scoring and that 'a base model frequently fixes these by proctoring (the Scoring move)' — i.e. the literal test points faithful users to the wrong link. Construct-grain dependence makes it worse: an unsupervised 'clinical reasoning' essay framed as 'articulate a reasoning process' → Scoring (oral probe); framed as 'reason like a clinician' → Construct underrepresentation (redesign) — identical artifact, opposite primary diagnoses and opposite first repairs, driven by an unstated construct-grain choice the test presupposes rather than resolves.
- **Verdict (real-issue, Medium):** The "Yes" branch encodes a necessary condition as sufficient and its take-home parenthetical (line 218→Scoring) flatly contradicts the pre-existing-weakness rule (141-143/387-388→Construct) on the same artifact inside the keystone file, inverting the primary diagnosis and first repair.
- **Proposed fix (advisory):** Fix three things in core/diagnostic-procedure.md. (1) Gate the "Yes" branch (lines 216-218) behind a CIV exclusion: "Yes AND the rubric pays out only for construct-relevant features → Scoring; captured honestly but only via AI-suppliable surface features (fluency/length/polish) → still Construct." This stops the test from stating a necessary condition as sufficient and mis-sorting the doc's own CIV example. (2) Resolve the direct contradiction over the unverifiable take-home write-up: the "Yes"-branch parenthetical (line 218) sorts it to Scoring while the pre-existing-weakness rule (141-143) and edge case (387-388) sort it to Construct. Delete or rewrite the take-home parenthetical and add one sentence giving the pre-existing-weakness rule explicit precedence ("If the task was weak evidence before AI, that is a Construct break regardless of the boundary test"). (3) Require the construct grain to be pinned as an explicit input before the boundary test may run (extend the Phase A sharpening step at 122-139 into a precondition), since varying the grain reverses the primary link and first repair. The G07/G11 ratification flags (golden-test-cases.md 414-421) should be kept and pointed at this fix.

#### B2 — B-boundary · **Medium** · document-limitation

- **Claim:** The Weakened/Severed scale — which deterministically sets Moderate vs Vulnerable readiness and light vs heavy repair — is defined entirely by two undefined words doing all the work in opposite directions: Severed = a non-doer 'could plausibly earn the same result'; Weakened = the result 'still mostly reflects the student's own intended work.' There is no quantitative bright line, no fraction-of-students, no effect size, no portion-of-score cutoff, no exemplars, and no inter-rater procedure — yet the doc treats this as a mechanical input and promises reproducibility (lines 4-5). 'Could plausibly earn the same result' is satisfied by essentially every unsupervised text artifact (collapsing Weakened to nonexistence for the whole take-home category), while 'mostly reflects the student's own work' has no operational test for how much AI contribution flips it. The definition is also interaction-laden: 'plausibly' is itself stakes-sensitive ('higher stakes lower the bar', line 158) and the proportion that is 'the student's own' under unverifiable AI use is, by the doc's own Gate 2, UNKNOWABLE — so the central classification turns on an unmeasurable proportion judged against an undefined, stakes-moving threshold.
- **Where:** core/diagnostic-procedure.md lines 150-162, 324-348, 478-479
- **Attack:** A take-home lab/data-analysis report where the student runs the experiment and the local dataset themselves but uses AI to draft ~60% of the prose. Director A: 'a non-doer could plausibly produce the same write-up cold from the dataset' → Severed → Vulnerable → rebuild with proctoring. Director B: 'the result mostly reflects her own work (she ran the experiment); AI shifted only the prose' → Weakened → Moderate → one-line AI-disclosure fix. Same case, same AI condition, opposite readiness and opposite repair burden, with nothing in the doc to adjudicate. For a tool whose value proposition is a reproducible, proportionate recommendation, the central severity call is undefined.
- **Verdict (document-limitation, Medium):** The attack as stated fails — G08 plus the boundary test already adjudicate its exact challenge case and name Director B's move as a disallowed repair — but it correctly surfaces a real, fixable under-specification: Weakened has no operational anchor symmetric to Severed, the adjudicating machinery is not co-located with the scale, and the Weakened band is undemonstrated for the take-home-text category, warranting documentation/coverage fixes rather than a redesign.
- **Proposed fix (advisory):** Make the Weakened/Severed scale self-adjudicating and demonstrably non-empty for unsupervised text. (1) At the Weakened/Severed bullets in core/diagnostic-procedure.md (lines 150-162), add a forward pointer to the boundary test (214-225) and an instruction to judge "mostly" against the construct, not the volume of text. (2) Give Weakened a symmetric operational anchor mirroring Severed, e.g. "AI changed form/efficiency but a non-doer still could NOT earn the same result, and the AI did not touch a scored part of the construct" — so both bands hang on the same "could a non-doer earn this?" lever. (3) Add at least one take-home/unsupervised-text golden case that lands Scoring-Weakened -> Moderate (e.g., a low-stakes artifact with a light existing attribution anchor), since the current take-home golden set (G08-G11) is uniformly Severed/Vulnerable, leaving the Weakened band for unverifiable text undemonstrated. Optionally state explicitly where the Weakened floor lives for unverifiable text. No change to the diagnostic logic or ordering is needed.

#### B4 — B-boundary · **Medium** · real-issue

- **Claim:** The 'Stakes are the single severity knob... there is no separate seriously-weakened tier — stakes move the one threshold' model (lines 158-162) is internally contradictory and not actually single or monotonic across its range. (1) It collides with the Needs-deeper-review override (lines 335-339): the override fires categorically whenever Stakes=consequential 'Independently of where the chain breaks,' overwriting the chain output, so the top of the stakes range is removed from the Severed/Vulnerable machinery — the artifact does not move smoothly Moderate→Vulnerable→Needs deeper review; there is a discontinuous hard override with an undefined high/consequential boundary. The system thus has TWO incompatible severity mechanisms (continuous threshold-shifting below consequential; categorical override at/above it) that can disagree. (2) The single knob collapses genuinely multidimensional stakes (grade weight vs gatekeeping function vs washback vs legal exposure — Messick's consequential validity is explicitly multidimensional) into one ordinal scalar, so it cannot represent a low-stakes-but-easily-gamed task versus a high-stakes-but-hard-to-game task, which need different repairs. (3) It contradicts the crisp, stakes-free Severed definition: whether 'a non-doer could plausibly earn the SAME result' is a property of the task and AI condition, true or false regardless of stakes — stakes cannot make a chatbot more or less able to produce the artifact.
- **Where:** core/diagnostic-procedure.md lines 150-162, 333-348
- **Attack:** A final worth 40% of a non-gating elective: line 333 lists 'high-stakes exams' under the override while line 338 says a 'normal summative course grade' is not consequential — no rule decides, so two evaluators route the identical exam to different tiers and response shapes. Conversely, the doc's own examples refute the 'single knob': Example 3/G03 is a proctored closed-book exam rated Strong at HIGH stakes (high stakes did NOT lower the bar — still Intact), while Example 1 is an identical-construct take-home rated Vulnerable at LOW stakes (low stakes did NOT raise it — still Severed). The artifact, not the stakes, decided both; stakes only move the needle in a narrow borderline band the doc refuses to define.
